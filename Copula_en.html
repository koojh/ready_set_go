1. Essence of Copulas: Separating Marginals from Dependence Structure

A copula is the core mathematical tool used in models such as Non-Parametric Bayesian Networks (NPBNs) to couple continuous, non-Gaussian hydrological variables into a unified multivariate joint distribution.

A copula is a linking function that completely separates each variable's individual probability distribution (its marginal distribution) from the joint statistical association among variables (the dependence structure). This allows zero-inflated, right-skewed rainfall ($\text{mm}$) and heavy-tailed river discharge ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$) to be modeled jointly without forcing either variable into a distorted normal distribution.

1.1 Sklar's Theorem

Let $F_1(x_1)$ and $F_2(x_2)$ be the individual cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) of continuous random variables $X_1$ and $X_2$, and let $F(x_1, x_2)$ be their joint CDF. Then there always exists a unique copula function $C$ linking the marginals to the joint distribution:

$$F(x_1, x_2) = C\big(F_1(x_1), \, F_2(x_2)\big) = C(u_1, u_2)$$
  • $u_1 = F_1(x_1) \in [0, 1]$,   $u_2 = F_2(x_2) \in [0, 1]$ (cumulative percentiles / non-exceedance probabilities of each variable)

This theorem generalizes directly to an $n$-dimensional multivariate network, decomposing the joint CDF of $n$ continuous random variables into their individual marginal CDFs $F_i(x_i)$ and an $n$-dimensional copula $C$:

$$F(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) = C\big(F_1(x_1), \, F_2(x_2), \, \dots, \, F_n(x_n)\big)$$

1.2 Probability Integral Transform

Regardless of how skewed or irregular the original distribution of a continuous random variable $X$ may be, transforming it through its own CDF into the cumulative percentile $U = F_X(X)$ always yields a standard uniform distribution $U \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$:

$$P(U \le u) = P(F_X(X) \le u) = P(X \le F_X^{-1}(u)) = F_X(F_X^{-1}(u)) = u \quad (0 \le u \le 1)$$
Core Intuition By temporarily stripping away physical units ($\text{mm}, \text{m}^3/\text{s}$) and asymmetric marginal shapes and mapping every variable onto the unit square $[0, 1]^2$, all variables sit on an identical uniform scale. What remains is purely the intrinsic joint co-movement pattern between variables.

2. Gaussian Copula and the 4-Step Probabilistic Inference Mechanism

The Gaussian copula models pure dependence in percentile space by borrowing the correlation structure of a multivariate normal distribution.

2.1 Gaussian Copula Formulation

$$C_\rho(u_1, u_2) = \Phi_\rho\left(\Phi^{-1}(u_1), \; \Phi^{-1}(u_2)\right)$$

Expressed in terms of the copula probability density function $c_\rho$ (where $z_i = \Phi^{-1}(u_i)$):

$$c_\rho(u_1, u_2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}} \exp\left( -\frac{\rho^2 z_1^2 - 2\rho z_1 z_2 + \rho^2 z_2^2}{2(1-\rho^2)} \right)$$
  • $\Phi^{-1}(\cdot)$: Inverse CDF (quantile function) of the standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$
  • $\Phi_\rho$: Bivariate standard normal CDF parameterized by correlation coefficient $\rho$

2.2 True Meaning of the Parameter $\rho$

  • Not the Pearson correlation of raw data: $\rho$ is not the direct linear correlation between observed rainfall ($\text{mm}$) and river discharge ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$).
  • Correlation in Latent Normal Space: It is the Pearson linear correlation ($\text{Corr}(Z_1, Z_2)$) between the latent normal scores $Z_1 = \Phi^{-1}(u_1)$ and $Z_2 = \Phi^{-1}(u_2)$ obtained after mapping percentiles through the inverse standard normal CDF.
  • Difference from a multivariate normal distribution: A multivariate normal model forces rainfall and discharge themselves to be Gaussian—producing physically impossible negative values—whereas the Gaussian copula preserves each variable's non-Gaussian empirical CDF 100% and uses the Gaussian structure solely to compute dependence.

2.3 4-Step Inference Pipeline for Probabilistic Inflow Forecasting

Given today's observed rainfall $x_1$, the pipeline for deriving the conditional probability distribution (and ensemble scenarios) of tomorrow's river discharge $Q$ operates in the following four steps:

STEP 1 Percentile Transformation of Observed Rainfall ($x_1 \to u_1$)

Evaluate the observed rainfall $x_1$ using the historical rainfall CDF ($F_1$) to obtain its percentile $u_1 = F_1(x_1) \in [0, 1]$. For example, if today's rain falls at the 12th percentile, then $u_1 = 0.12$.

STEP 2 Projection to Standard Normal Space & Conditional Inference ($u_1 \to z_1 \to Z_2$)

Map the percentile $u_1$ through the inverse standard normal CDF to obtain the latent normal score $z_1 = \Phi^{-1}(u_1)$ ($u_1 = 0.12 \implies z_1 \approx -1.17$). By the conditional probability law of the bivariate normal distribution, the latent variable $Z_2$ for tomorrow's discharge conditioned on $Z_1 = z_1$ follows an exact 1-D Gaussian distribution:

$$Z_2 \mid (Z_1 = z_1) \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\rho z_1, \; 1 - \rho^2\right)$$

Note: The discharge percentile $U_2$ itself is not normal; rather, its normal-score transformation $Z_2 = \Phi^{-1}(U_2)$ follows a Gaussian distribution.

STEP 3 Returning to Discharge Percentiles via Standard Normal CDF ($Z_2 \to U_2$)

Pass the conditional $Z_2$ distribution (or Monte Carlo samples drawn from it) back through the standard normal CDF $\Phi(\cdot)$. This maps values on $(-\infty, +\infty)$ back onto $[0, 1]$, yielding the conditional percentile distribution of discharge $U_2 = \Phi(Z_2)$.

STEP 4 Restoring Physical Units via Historical Inverse Discharge CDF ($U_2 \to Q$)

Apply the historical inverse discharge CDF (quantile function $F_2^{-1}$) to $U_2$ to obtain the forecast distribution in physical units ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$): $Q = F_2^{-1}(U_2)$. In this final step, Gaussian symmetry disappears and the basin's true asymmetry and heavy right tail are restored.

Structural Limitation of the Gaussian Copula (Zero Tail Dependence) Mathematically, whenever $\rho \lt 1$, both the upper and lower tail dependence coefficients of a Gaussian copula equal zero ($\lambda_U = \lambda_L = 0$). Because joint extreme events become asymptotically independent, the Gaussian copula can slightly underestimate simultaneous extreme flood peaks during severe rainstorms.

3. Relationship with Spearman's Rank Correlation ($r$)

Spearman's rank correlation and copulas are not competing alternatives. The copula serves as the probabilistic engine (the functional framework) for conditional inference and scenario sampling, while Spearman's rank correlation serves as the empirical measurement and calibration tool that populates the copula parameter $\rho$ from data.

Dimension Spearman's Rank Correlation ($r$ or $\rho_s$) Copula ($C(u_1, u_2)$)
Mathematical Form A single scalar number ($-1 \le r \le 1$) A full multidimensional joint probability surface
Primary Role A summary statistic capturing monotonic co-movement between two variables A complete probabilistic model encoding joint dependence across all percentiles
Simulation Capability Cannot generate future scenarios from a single scalar alone Enables conditional distribution inversion and Monte Carlo ensemble sampling

3.1 Closed-Form Transformation Identity

For a Gaussian copula, an exact one-to-one analytical identity links the rank-space Spearman correlation $r$ of the observed data to the latent-normal-space Pearson correlation $\rho$:

$$r = \frac{6}{\pi}\arcsin\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \rho = 2\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}r\right)$$

Consequently, instead of running iterative numerical optimization (such as maximum likelihood estimation), one only needs to compute Spearman's rank correlation $r$ from observations and apply a single trigonometric evaluation to determine the Gaussian copula parameter $\rho$.

Next Step: Application to Bayesian Networks (BN) Together, the copula separation of marginals from dependence and the closed-form identity $\rho = 2\sin(\frac{\pi}{6}r)$ form the core engine that quantifies arcs in continuous Non-Parametric Bayesian Networks (NPBNs) and Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBNs).
