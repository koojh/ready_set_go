Part 01 · Overall Architecture

1. Overall Transformer Blueprint & Where Self-Attention Fits

The Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) replaces recurrence and convolutions with Self-Attention, assembling it together with Positional Encoding, Position-wise Feed-Forward Networks (FFN/MLP), and Add & LayerNorm blocks. Originally proposed for sequence-to-sequence translation, it consists of an Encoder (which reads the input) and a Decoder (which generates the output). Modern GPT-style LLMs use a stack of Decoder blocks alone.

Part 02 · Transformer Layer

2. What Exactly Is a "Transformer Layer"?

Just as a single Linear → ReLU block forms one layer in a standard neural network, a Transformer Layer (or Transformer Block) is a standardized modular unit combining contextual aggregation (Attention) and non-linear feature transformation (FFN).

Inside One Encoder Layer (4 Steps)

  1. Multi-Head Self-Attention (Context Gatherer): Each token attends to all other positions to gather weighted contextual information. This is the only operation where information flows across different token positions.
  2. Add & Norm (First Stabilization): Adds the residual input $X$ back to the attention output and normalizes across the embedding dimension:
    $$H = \text{LayerNorm}\big(X + \text{MultiHeadAttention}(X)\big)$$
  3. Position-wise FFN / MLP (Feature Processor): Applies the exact same 2-layer MLP independently to every row (token position) of $H$:
    $$\text{FFN}(x) = \max(0, x W_1 + b_1) W_2 + b_2$$
  4. Add & Norm (Second Stabilization): Adds $H$ back to the FFN output and normalizes again:
    $$X_{\text{out}} = \text{LayerNorm}\big(H + \text{FFN}(H)\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}$$
Why Stack $N$ Identical Layers (6, 12, or 96 Layers)?
  • Shape Invariance ($L \times d_{\text{model}}$): Because every Transformer layer outputs a matrix of the exact same shape as its input, layers can be stacked vertically like Lego bricks (each layer learns its own independent weight matrices).
  • Hierarchical Abstraction: Lower layers (Layers 1–2) capture local syntactic patterns and word-level dependencies, while higher layers combine those contextualized features to capture long-range coreference, intent, and abstract reasoning.
Part 03 · Layer Normalization

3. How LayerNorm Works (vs. Input Scaling & BatchNorm)

Layer Normalization (LayerNorm) is not a pre-processing step applied to raw dataset inputs; it is an internal normalization layer that keeps hidden-state activations well-scaled inside every block.

Property Batch Normalization Layer Normalization
Normalization Axis Computes mean and variance across the batch ($B$) for each feature channel Computes mean and variance across the $d_{\text{model}}$ features within each single token ($i$)
Batch & Length Sensitivity Unstable for small batch sizes or variable sequence lengths Completely independent of batch size and sequence length $L$
Equation For token $i$'s vector $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}$ with mean $\mu_i$ and std $\sigma_i$:
$$\hat{x}_{i, m} = \frac{x_{i, m} - \mu_i}{\sqrt{\sigma_i^2 + \epsilon}}, \qquad y_{i, m} = \gamma_m \hat{x}_{i, m} + \beta_m$$

Repeatedly adding residual branches (`X + Sublayer(X)`) across dozens of layers can cause activation magnitudes to drift or explode. LayerNorm stabilizes vector norms at every sub-layer so gradients flow smoothly during training.

Part 04 · Causal Masking

4. Masked Self-Attention & the $-\infty$ Masking Trick

During autoregressive generation, the Decoder produces tokens one by one from left to right. While training feeds the entire target sentence in parallel for efficiency, position $i$ must never peek at future tokens ($j > i$).

Given a $3 \times 3$ scaled score matrix $S = \frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}$, we mask out upper-triangular entries ($j > i$) by adding $-\infty$:

$$S = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 & 2.5 & 0.8 \\ 0.4 & 1.2 & 3.1 \\ 1.5 & 0.9 & 2.0 \end{bmatrix} \quad \xrightarrow{\text{Mask future entries } (j > i)\text{ with } -\infty} \quad S_{\text{masked}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 & -\infty & -\infty \\ 0.4 & 1.2 & -\infty \\ 1.5 & 0.9 & 2.0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Applying row-wise Softmax turns every $-\infty$ entry into $\exp(-\infty) = 0$, guaranteeing zero weight on future tokens:

$$A_{\text{masked}} = \text{softmax}(S_{\text{masked}}) \approx \begin{bmatrix} 1.000 & 0.000 & 0.000 \\ 0.310 & 0.690 & 0.000 \\ 0.313 & 0.172 & 0.515 \end{bmatrix}$$
Part 05 · Cross-Attention

5. Cross-Attention: Connecting Encoder and Decoder

Why does the Decoder need an explicit Cross-Attention sub-layer instead of relying on the FFN (MLP) to connect source and target sentences?

  • Why FFN Cannot Do This: A Position-wise FFN operates solely on a single Decoder token vector $x_i$ at a time—it has no access to the $L_{\text{in}}$ source tokens encoded by the Encoder.
  • How Cross-Attention Bridges Both Sequences:
    • Query ($Q$): Generated from the Decoder's current hidden states ($L_{\text{out}} \times d_k$).
    • Key ($K$) & Value ($V$): Generated from the Encoder's final output $H_{\text{enc}}$ ($L_{\text{in}} \times d_k$, $L_{\text{in}} \times d_v$).
    Computing $Q_{\text{dec}} K_{\text{enc}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{L_{\text{out}} \times L_{\text{in}}}$ allows every target token being generated to dynamically attend to the most relevant source words.
Part 06 · Why Not Plain DNN?

6. Why Plain DNNs Fail & How Variable Length $L$ Works

Key Question: "If we feed word vectors in order into a standard DNN, wouldn't it already know both order and content? And if sequence length $L$ changes, shouldn't $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ change size too?"

1) Why Plain DNNs Struggle with Sequences

  • Rigid Slot Binding: A plain DNN assigns separate weights $W_1, W_2, \dots, W_L$ to fixed input slots. If a model learns "The dog chased the cat" with "dog" in slot 1, shifting it to slot 4 ("Yesterday in the park, the dog...") forces slot 4's weights $W_4$ to relearn the pattern from scratch.
  • Static vs. Dynamic Routing: A DNN's connection weights are frozen after training, whereas contextual links (e.g., whether "it" refers to "animal" or "street") must be computed dynamically for each input sentence.

2) Why $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ Keep the Exact Same Shape Regardless of $L$

The projection matrices $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ do not multiply across the sequence length $L$; they transform the feature dimension $d_{\text{model}}$ of each individual token row:

$$X \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}, \qquad W_Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad Q = X W_Q \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_k}$$

Because matrix multiplication only requires matching inner dimensions ($d_{\text{model}}$), changing $L$ from 3 to 5 leaves $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ completely unchanged while automatically producing a $5 \times 5$ attention matrix $A$ and a $5 \times d_v$ output matrix $Z$.

Part 07 · Dimensions & Output Heads

7. Sequence Length ($L$), Token Dimension ($d_{\text{model}}$), and Output Heads

Viewing the input matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}$ as a table:

  • Sequence Length ($L$): Number of tokens/positions in the sequence = number of rows.
  • Token Dimension ($d_{\text{model}}$): Length of the feature vector representing a single token = number of columns.

Because the final output $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}$ has $L$ rows, downstream prediction heads handle it according to the task:

  • Next-Token Prediction (LLMs / Decoders): Every row $z_i$ encodes context up to step $i$ to predict token $i+1$. A shared linear layer (Linear(d_model, vocab_size)) is applied row-by-row to produce next-token probabilities at every position.
  • Sequence Classification (Sentiment, Topic): To output a single class label for the whole sequence, the variable-length $L \times d_{\text{model}}$ matrix is collapsed into a fixed $1 \times d_{\text{model}}$ vector via Mean Pooling ($\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^L z_i$) or by extracting a special summary token (such as [CLS] or the final token $z_L$).
